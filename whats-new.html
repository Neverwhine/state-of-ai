Models

The model market became a barbell — and “Frontier of Restraint” is retired

Prices moved in both directions in one quarter. Claude Fable 5 / Mythos 5 pushed the top end up to $10/$50 for multi-day agentic work; Sonnet 5 (promo $2/$10 through Aug 31, then $3/$15, 1M context, now the Claude Code default) and Grok 4.5 ($2/$6, 500K context, co-trained with Cursor) pulled frontier-adjacent capability down. GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna reached GA July 9 at $5/$30, $2.50/$15 and $1/$6, with Sol 54% more token-efficient on coding. Meta shipped its first paid Model API (Muse Spark 1.1, $1.25/$4.25) and Gemini 3.6 Flash cut output pricing to $1.50/$7.50 with 17% fewer output tokens. Chinese open weights took the parameter crown: Kimi K3 at 2.8T (weights Jul 27), Qwen3.8-Max-Preview at 2.4T, DeepSeek V4 Pro at 1.6T. Restricted/cyber remains a live secondary narrative — a 19-day global suspension of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 and a US pre-release review before GPT-5.6 GA. We also added the benchmark-integrity caveat: the Grok 4.5 CursorBench result was withdrawn after training-data contamination.

Healthcare AI

Healthcare AI substantially updated: AI follows incentives — efficiency and total spend both go up

The section’s first major revision since it shipped, plus a new synthesis slide. The reframing: ambient documentation genuinely saves clinician time (−13 to −16 min/day in a 5-hospital JAMA study) and health plans project a 9% group / 8.5% individual 2027 medical cost trend — the highest in ~two decades — with ~70% ranking provider AI documentation and coding among their top three cost inflators. The mechanism is measured, not asserted: one maternity code rose from 4% to 12.3% of admissions while transfusions barely moved, adding $22M; Blue Health Intelligence ties ~$2.3B of extra expected spend to AI-enabled documentation; UCSF found more billing units per encounter with no increase in denials. Also added: a prior-auth AI-vs-AI node (WISeR survived the Senate 46–50; the Protecting Patients from Automated Denials Act; a converging licensed-human-signoff + disclosure + reporting standard; ePA mandatory Jan 1, 2027); the consumer front door (Health in ChatGPT launched Jul 23 with Apple Health, hospital records, One Medical and Function connections; 40M users ask health questions daily; 1 in 7 UK adults used AI instead of a doctor); safety and liability as the second gate (NOHARM’s 76.6% omissions — not peer-reviewed and disputed; OpenAI sued for the unauthorized practice of medicine one day before launch; 1.4M patients exposed across 7 health systems in an AI vendor breach); two clocks replacing one horizon (admin 2–4 years on deadlines, clinical autonomy 10–15 years on evidence and liability — 43% piloting agents but only 3% live, 68% pre-scale, FDA zero marketed genAI devices: designations, not clearances); refreshed CMS Year 2 workstreams; the NHS single-payer counterfactual; healthcare funding concentration; and portfolio updates for Doctronic (acquired Summer Health for 100K+ pediatric interactions) and Collectly (live on the Epic Showroom, 3,000+ facilities, 475K+ billing questions resolved). All vendor metrics are now explicitly labelled company-disclosed. Money-river and patient-loop interactions are unchanged.

Regulation

EU AI Act enforcement went live August 2 — Article 50 binds deployers, not just labs

Chatbot disclosure, deepfake labelling and machine-readable marking of AI-generated content are enforceable across the EU as of Aug 2, 2026. Fine tiers matter and are frequently conflated: €15M / 3% for GPAI provider non-compliance, with €35M / 7% reserved for prohibited practices. The expensive high-risk conformity work slipped to Dec 2, 2027 and Aug 2, 2028. In the US, EO 14409 created a voluntary up-to-30-day pre-release review channel that has already produced a 19-day global suspension of two frontier models, and the White House launched GOLD EAGLE on July 14. Export controls are now bilateral and extraterritorial. Sovereign AI passed >$350B committed, with MGX Fund I at $49B, a $40B Aligned Data Centers buyout, and Kuwait’s Helix at $10B+. Capital rotated to implementation, not models: Anthropic’s Ode and Thrive’s ~$2B raise. Report-only — no new slide.

NIST/CAISI eval: DeepSeek V4 Pro about 8 months behind US frontier on held-out benchmarks

CAISI's independent evaluation used non-public ("uncontaminated") benchmarks across cyber, software engineering, sciences, abstract reasoning, and math. Business Models

Outcome pricing is the direction; hybrid usage pricing is the reality

Coatue's "services as software" framing reads AI as a re-pricing of the software stack from per-seat to per-output, with the cited 3.8% consumption share of traditional SaaS as the gap between narrative and commercial reality. Orb's State of AI Agent Pricing reinforces the same point from a billing-system vantage: most production agent companies are on hybrid pricing today (subscription floor plus usage and outcome components). Outcome pricing is the strategic direction, hybrid/usage pricing is current commercial reality, and subscriptions are increasingly used as a revenue floor inside hybrid models rather than as the value-capture engine.

Cloud

OpenAI / Microsoft exclusivity reset — and OpenAI's multi-cloud stress

Under the Apr 27 partnership reset, Microsoft is no longer OpenAI's exclusive cloud and the IP license through 2032 is non-exclusive; OpenAI continues a (capped) revenue share to Microsoft through 2030 while Microsoft no longer pays revenue share to OpenAI. The strategic cost of cutting the tether is real: OpenAI now has to assemble compute and distribution from multiple patrons (Microsoft, Oracle, AWS, plus its own buildout) without a single dominant protector. The Incumbents section now frames OpenAI/Microsoft/Oracle/AWS as one of three coalitions under stress, alongside the Anthropic/Amazon/Google/SpaceX/Cursor diversified-but-interdependent stack and Meta's separate vertically controlled stack.
